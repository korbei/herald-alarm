Privacy

Your morning stays on your phone.

No ads, no account, no analytics. Herald only talks to the outside world for the features you switch on.

Read the privacy policy
  • Alarms, settings, recordings and your calendar never leave the device.
  • Weather sends an approximate location — or a place name you typed — to Open-Meteo, and nothing else.
  • News fetches a public feed; no identifiers are sent.
  • AI voices send the text to be spoken to the provider you chose, with your own key, which is stored encrypted on the phone.
  • Purchases go through Google Play; Herald never sees your payment details.
FAQ

Questions, answered.

Do I need an AI account to use Herald?

No. Herald uses the voices already on your phone, for free, with no sign-up. AI voices are an option for Pro users who have their own ElevenLabs, OpenAI or Google Cloud account — you paste your key, Herald sends only the text to speak, and the provider bills you directly for what you use.

Does it work offline?

Alarms, spoken messages with device voices, recordings and your own music all work with no connection. Weather, news and AI voices are fetched ahead of time and cached, so a flaky morning connection usually doesn't matter; if something couldn't be fetched, the alarm still rings and the device voice reads your message.

What happens if my Pro ends?

Your alarms keep ringing exactly as before. The Pro extras — AI voices, agenda, weather, news, audio-file alarms — pause until Pro is active again, and the alarm list shows which alarms are affected. Nothing is deleted.

How does the free trial work?

Start the trial from the paywall; Google Play handles it. You get seven days of Pro, then the plan you picked renews unless you cancel in Google Play's Subscriptions page at least 24 hours before the trial ends. One trial per Google account.

Which permissions does Herald ask for, and why?

Exact alarms and notifications to ring; calendar only for the agenda; microphone only to record a message; approximate location only for weather with your location. Everything else is optional and explained in the privacy policy.

Which phones are supported?

Android 12 and newer. Herald is built with Android's exact-alarm and full-screen-intent APIs so it rings on the lock screen like a system alarm.
